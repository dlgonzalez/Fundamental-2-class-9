Grammar focus · Lesson 2

There is / There are

Say what exists in a place, what doesn't exist and ask about places in a neighborhood.

1 · Basic forms

There is
One thing / singular noun.
There is a library.
There's a bus stop.
There are
More than one / plural noun.
There are two parks.
There are some cafés.
Negative forms
There isn't a gym. = There is not a gym.
There aren't any parks. = There are not any parks.
There is no library. · There are no parks.

2 · Questions and short answers

Is there a coffee shop near here?
Yes, there is. / No, there isn't.
Are there any grocery stores in your neighborhood?
Yes, there are. / No, there aren't.

Use Is there...? for one thing and Are there...? for plural things.

3 · Quick grammar check

4 · Correct the mistakes

5 · Describe a neighborhood

My neighborhood is quiet and friendly. There is a small grocery store near my house. There are two busy streets and a beautiful park. There isn't a library, but there are three coffee shops. I think it's a convenient place to live.

More examples

The city is busy and exciting.
My street is quiet and clean.
There is a modern hospital.
The park is beautiful and peaceful.
The bus station is crowded and noisy.
My apartment is small but comfortable.

Useful sentence starters

Speaking production · Final challenge

Design & describe a neighborhood

Work with a partner. Imagine you are designing a neighborhood for new residents. Include places, describe the area and explain what it needs.

Step 1 · Build your map

Use this sample map as a starting point, or draw your own in your notebook. Your neighborhood must have at least six places.

Step 2 · Describe it

Prepare a short description. Include:

Example: “Our neighborhood is modern and lively. There is a large park near the school. There are two grocery stores and a gym. There isn't a hospital. We need a clinic because many families live here.”

Step 3 · Ask your partner

Don't look at your partner's map. Ask questions to discover what places are there.

A: Is there a library?
B: Yes, there is. / No, there isn't.
A: Are there any restaurants?
B: Yes, there are two.
A: Is there a gym near the park?
B: No, there isn't. It's next to the school.
